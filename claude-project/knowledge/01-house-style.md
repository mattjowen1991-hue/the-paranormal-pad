# House Style - how Incident Reports and Tapes are written

This is the editorial standard for The Paranormal Pad, drawn from Matt's eight published
reports. Anything that drafts, edits or builds a report follows it. When in doubt, read
Report 008 ("Come and play with us") and Report 006 ("Letters on the Board") - they are the
best current examples of the voice.

---

## 1. The voice in one paragraph

Matt writes like a curious, level-headed mate telling you something that genuinely happened
to someone he knows - over a pint, not from a pulpit. First person, warm, British, a bit
self-aware. He is a believer who works hard to be fair: he tells the story straight, lets the
witness speak, then turns the torch on it himself ("How can we debunk this?") before admitting
what still doesn't add up. Suspense comes from facts and pacing, not adjectives. He never
claims more than the witness saw.

## 2. Who is speaking

- **Matt's framing is always first person** ("I", "my Nan", "my partner"). He is "The Reporter".
- **The witness's account is told in the witness's own first person**, in its own section
  ("## Fran's Story", "## Lou's Story", "## Talita's Story"), introduced by Matt with a line such
  as "What follows is told from Fran's perspective." When the witness is Matt himself, the whole
  report is his first person.
- **Talk to the reader directly** now and then: "For those unfamiliar, ...", "If you've ever
  navigated a blended family, you know...", "Imagine trying to anticipate a voice you know you
  can't physically hear anymore."

## 3. British, plain, specific

- British spelling and words: sceptic, colour, realise, neighbour, Mum, Nan, Grandad, council
  estate, box room, petrol station, "a load of rubbish". Explain very British things briefly for
  overseas readers ("a council estate in the UK is somewhat akin to the projects in the USA").
- Concrete details over mood words: the Most Haunted barrel episode, the VHS camcorder, the Ring
  doorbell, the scout uniform, the smell of a pipe, an exact road name, a year, an age ("I was
  around 13 or 14 (at the time of writing, I'm 35)").
- Describe layouts precisely when place matters: "You'd walk through the front door into a small
  hallway with the stairs straight ahead and a door to the left..." - readers should be able to
  picture where everyone was.
- Honest about memory: "as far as I can recall", "I honestly can't remember it properly, so I'd
  say with confidence that it only happened once." Uncertainty is stated, never smoothed over.

## 4. Rhythm and suspense

- Mostly medium-length, easy paragraphs, then **short one-line beats at the turning points**:
  "Nothing happened." / "Silence." / "And then I saw it." / "Caroline was tragically killed in the wreckage."
- Dialogue is quoted and kept short and real, including the dull bits that make it believable
  ("OK."). Words heard or spoken during the event, and non-English words, go in *italics*;
  translations in brackets: *"Uphi?"* (Where are you?).
- Build to the event in order: normal life -> first small oddity -> escalation -> the peak -> after.
- **Adjectives are the enemy of scary.** "It was completely black, like a shadow, with no facial
  features" beats "a terrifying, horrifying demonic shape". One strong image per moment.

## 5. The shape of a report

Use the sections the story needs; this is the usual order.

1. **Opening / `## Report Introduction`** - how the story reached Matt and why it stuck with him.
   Who told it (relationship, a line about their character - especially if they are a sceptic),
   how he came to interview them, and any consent/anonymity note ("She has asked to remain
   anonymous, which I completely understand, so ... we'll call her Fran."). Matt's own stance up
   front, often his hesitation or logical instinct.
2. **The place** (`### The House`, or woven in) - where, when it was built, who lived there
   before, anything relevant in its history (researched: old maps, records, local history).
   Photos: Google Maps, old maps, the layout.
3. **The account** - either
   - `## Name's Story` with `### Part 1: Short Title`, `### Part 2: ...` for one escalating event, or
   - `### Incident 1`, `### Incident 2` ... for a series of separate happenings, each opening with
     where/when and closing with how it left them ("It's important to note that this has never
     happened again since that night.").
   Small sub-titles inside a section use `####` (bold italic).
4. **Follow-up / investigation** (`## Report Follow-Up`, `## The Investigation`) - what Matt
   checked afterwards: asking other witnesses, records, MOD files, maps, genealogy. Say plainly
   what couldn't be found and why.
5. **Addendum** (`## Addendum: ...`) - related but separate memories, clearly flagged, with a line
   on why they're included.
6. **`## My Thoughts`** (or `## Lou's Thoughts` when the witness gives theirs) - Matt's honest
   read. Always:
   - gives the **mundane explanation its fair due** by name (sleep paralysis, hypnopompic
     hallucination, headlights, coincidence, grief, how family stories drift over 80 years), and
     says whether he thinks it's the most likely one;
   - then says **specifically what doesn't fit** ("She sat up and moved freely, she held a
     back-and-forth conversation...") - "None of that proves anything, but...";
   - may connect to wider lore (British fae tradition, Jacques Vallee's *Passport to Magonia*,
     UAP/NHI accounts, similar cases) - clearly as musing, not evidence;
   - ends with a **question to the reader** and an invitation: "What do you think? ... leave your
     witness statement below if you've ever had a similar experience." (The site calls comments
     **witness statements** - say that, not "comments".)

## 6. Honesty rules (non-negotiable)

- **Never invent** a fact, date, name, quote, record, source or detail. If the notes don't say
  it, ask Matt or leave it out. If research can't confirm something, say so in the text.
- Keep the witness's words and meaning; tidy grammar, never change what happened.
- Anonymity: use exactly the pseudonyms and details Matt approves. Note that a name has been
  changed the first time it appears.
- AI-generated images are labelled in the caption ("AI recreation of the scene").
- Sensitive history (abuse, illness, death) - mention only what the story needs, gently, and say
  when something "isn't my story to speculate on".
- Don't oversell. "I have no idea what it was" is a perfectly good sentence.

## 7. Titles, excerpts and captions

- **Title**: short and evocative, often a phrase from the story itself - "Come and play with us",
  "Letters on the Board", "The Call from Show Village", "The Medford Shadow". Sentence case or
  title case as reads best; no clickbait, no "SHOCKING".
- **Excerpt** (cards and WhatsApp previews): the first 1-2 sentences of the report, so the
  opening paragraph should hook on its own and not start with a heading-like phrase.
- **Photo captions**: what it is, then the source: "Astbury Avenue, Smethwick. Image: Google Maps",
  "The Administration Block. Image: County Asylums", "AI recreation of the scene". No long URLs
  in captions - link the source name instead.

## 8. Formatting and punctuation

- **No em dashes** (or en dashes used as dashes). Use a comma, brackets, a full stop, or a plain
  hyphen with spaces if it really is needed.
- Curly quotes are fine; keep them consistent.
- Numbers: "around 9 or 10", "the early hours", years as numbers, "the 1940s".
- Bold is rare - a key rule ("**Do not mess around with Ouija boards.**") or a date that matters.
- Headings: `##` main sections (shown with a diamond divider), `###` parts/incidents (red, dashed
  line), `####` small bold-italic titles.

## 9. Length

Let the story set it. Published reports run from about 650 words (a single sleep-paralysis
episode) to 5,000 (a months-long haunting). Most land between 1,500 and 4,000. Cut anything that
repeats; never pad.

## 10. Words and habits to watch

- Matt's recurring phrases are fine in moderation: "It's important to note", "For context",
  "To set the scene", "As always, my instinct is...", "I'd love to hear what you think".
- AI-sounding filler is not: "delve" (use at most once), "tapestry", "testament to", "a chilling
  reminder", "little did they know", "sent shivers down my spine" more than once, stacked
  adjectives, and neat "it wasn't X, it was Y" flourishes.
- Don't end every paragraph on a cliffhanger. Let ordinary moments be ordinary.

## 11. Incident Tapes

A tape page is short. It has:
- the video (automatic),
- `## Narrated by {Channel}` - one paragraph: who the channel is (linked), which of Matt's
  reports they narrated, the video's title in *italics* and publish date, "shared here with
  {Channel}'s permission", plus view counts if notable;
- one line on what the tape plays: "My story starts at 05:15 and runs until 30:25. This tape
  plays just that part." (or one side each for a two-sided tape);
- `### {Report title}` (or `### Side A: ...`) - a 1-2 sentence summary taken from the report,
  no spoilers beyond the report's own opening, then `[PLAY SIDE A]`;
- `## Credits` - narration, art (as credited in the video description), and "Story by The
  Reporter", with how Matt was credited on the video (e.g. u/MattJowen on Reddit).

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] Nothing invented; every fact is in Matt's notes or checked.
- [ ] Anonymity and pseudonyms exactly as agreed.
- [ ] Opening hooks on its own (it becomes the excerpt).
- [ ] Mundane explanation named fairly in My Thoughts.
- [ ] Ends with a question and a pointer to witness statements.
- [ ] No em dashes; British spelling.
- [ ] Every [PHOTO n] has a picture and a caption with its source; AI images labelled.
- [ ] Subjects chosen from the site's list.
